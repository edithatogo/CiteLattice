# Proposed study: research signals, innovation diffusion and VOI

Research question: do coverage-aware, as-of-knowledge bibliometric and research-system signals improve forecasts of technology adoption and subsequent research allocation compared with publication/citation count baselines?

Freeze historical observation cutoffs, source and method versions, technology partitions and held-out adoption data before analysis. Baselines: count-only, trend-only and historical diffusion curves. Models: logistic, Bass, Gompertz, substitution/competition variants via **Innovate**, optionally using network and topic signals from CiteLattice. Evaluate forecast error, calibration, leakage, coverage sensitivity, cost and stability.

Only after a valid decision problem is specified, use **Voiage** to estimate VOI under explicit alternatives, outcome-dependent utilities, joint uncertainty, study design, time horizon and population. A citation count alone is not a utility or net benefit. Compare study designs using valid method-specific EVPI/EVPPI/EVSI/ENBS rather than assumed generic calls.

Potential consumer paths: Kairos optional for discrete-event implementation scenarios, Paignia optional for strategic responses, Searchright for living evidence lineage. No circular Rust dependency; pass typed Arrow/Parquet and versioned manifests.

No data acquisition, external publication or methodological superiority claim follows merely from this design. Independent outcomes and held-out labels are essential.
